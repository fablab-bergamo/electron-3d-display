Multi-Electronic Atoms

The physics behind every element's electron cloud: what the atomSFE radial tables actually contain, how ground-state electron configuration and Hund's rule drive the cloud's shape, and how effective nuclear charge fills the gap for elements beyond Z = 92.

Scope

This document covers the physics models the atom pipeline rests on — what u(r) means, why full shells are isotropic, why Hund's rule applies to partial shells, and how Slater's effective-nuclear-charge substitution works for Z > 92. The step-by-step computation (which code runs, in what order) is in Tables to Point Clouds; how the blobs are built and flashed is in Tables to BIN; the technical status and validation numbers live in ATOMS.md.

What the radial tables encode — u(r) = r · R(r)

pc/hfs_atomsfe.py · data/hfs_tables.bin — one row per occupied (n, ℓ) subshell, Z = 1..92

Given — the N-electron problem

Ĥ Ψ = E Ψ,   Ĥ = Σi (−12∇i² − Zri) + Σi<j 1rij

The exact many-body Hamiltonian couples all N electrons together via the 1/rij repulsion. It is not separable — no analytic solution exists for Z > 1. All units are Hartree atomic units (ℏ = me = e = 1).

Kohn–Sham DFT

1 · Kohn–Sham mean-field reduction

[−12∇² + Veff[ρ](r)] φi(r) = εi φi(r)
ρ(r) = Σi ni |φi(r)|²

Kohn–Sham DFT replaces the interacting N-electron problem with N independent single-particle equations in a self-consistent effective potential Veff. The equations must be solved iteratively (SCF loop) because Veff depends on the density ρ, which depends on the orbitals φi. SPARC-atomSFE converges this loop using spectral finite elements with LDA_SVWN exchange-correlation.

pc/_atomsfe_vendor / pc/hfs_atomsfe.py
Veff = Vnuc + VH + Vxc

2 · The effective potential — three contributions

Vnuc(r) = −Zr    (nuclear attraction, dominant at small r)
VH(r) = ∫ ρ(r′)|r−r′| d³r′    (Hartree: mean electron-electron repulsion)
Vxc(r) = δ Exc[ρ]δ ρ(r)    (LDA Slater–Vosko–Wilk–Nusair exchange-correlation)

Vnuc is exact and analytic. VH is computed self-consistently on the radial grid (Poisson equation). Vxc is the only approximation: LDA_SVWN treats the exchange-correlation energy density as that of a uniform electron gas at the local density ρ(r) — accurate for core shells, slightly over-delocalizing for valence orbitals (the standard LDA self-interaction error, which is why the LDA valence radii are larger than Hartree-Fock Clementi-Raimondi values).

LDA_SVWN — local density approximation
spherical atom

3 · Spherical symmetry — radial reduction and u(r)

φn,ℓ,m(r,θ,φ) = Rn,ℓ(r) · Yℓm(θ,φ)
substitute   un,ℓ(r) = r · Rn,ℓ(r)
[−12d²dr² + ℓ(ℓ+1)2r² + Veff(r)] u(r) = ε · u(r)
boundary conditions: u(0) = 0,   u(r) → 0 as r → ∞

The angular factor Yℓm separates exactly for a spherical Veff(r). The substitution u = r·R eliminates the first-derivative term from the radial equation, turning it into a standard 1D Schrödinger form — the same shape as a particle in a potential, easy to solve numerically with finite elements or Numerov integration. The ℓ(ℓ+1)/(2r²) term is the centrifugal barrier: it pushes electrons with higher angular momentum away from the nucleus (p and d orbitals have no probability at r = 0).

1D Schrödinger form — basis of SPARC-atomSFE
u(r) on a grid

4 · Why u(r)² is the probability we integrate

∫ |φ|² d³r = ∫0∞ r² |R(r)|² dr · ∫ |Yℓm|² dΩ = ∫0∞ u(r)² dr
P(r) = u(r)²   — radial probability density
normalization:   ∫0∞ u(r)² dr = 1

u(r)² already folds in the r² Jacobian of spherical coordinates — the same reason the hydrogen tables use [r·R(r)]² as their radial weight. The table row for a given (Z, n, ℓ) is therefore directly proportional to the probability of finding that subshell's electron at radius r; integrating it gives the inverse-CDF used for sampling. No extra r² factor is needed at draw time.

radial probability density — same as [r·R(r)]² in the hydrogen pipeline
grid choice

5 · The log-uniform radial grid

ri = rmin · (rmaxrmin)i/(N−1),   rmin = 10−6 Bohr,   rmax = 100 Bohr
N = 2001 in the full npz  →  128 in the device blob (log-subsampled)

A log-uniform grid has a constant ratio between consecutive steps — Δr/r is constant — instead of a constant absolute step Δr. This is essential: the wavefunction oscillates on a scale of ~10⁻³ Bohr near a heavy nucleus (where u(r) rises sharply from zero), and decays exponentially over tens of Bohr in the valence region. A linear grid would either miss the core or waste 99% of its points on the tail. At 128 device points, the log-spacing keeps the mode radius accurate to better than 0.1% (measured against the 2001-point reference).

log-uniform spacing — r_min = 1e-6, r_max = 100 Bohr

Deliverable — hfs_tables.bin rows

• One row u(r)[128] per occupied (Z, n, ℓ) subshell, Z = 1..92
• Shared log-uniform grid r[128] (Bohr), one copy for all Z
• Header: gridSize = 128, elementCount = 92, subshellCount = 915

The table is indexed by Z (element offset + count) and then by subshell (n, ℓ). To get R(r), divide each row element by the corresponding r: R(r) = u(r)/r. The angular part Yℓm is not in the table — it is shared across all Z and n, and is handled by the compile-time angular library (see Coefficients to Tables and the atom pipeline's step 2).

1 · Ground-state electron configuration

Madelung order: fill in ascending (n+ℓ), break ties by lower n
1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, …
Z electrons  →  {(n, ℓ, occ)}   with real exceptions applied

Madelung's rule gives the right filling order for most elements — it is a consequence of orbital energies being set more by n+ℓ than by n alone, due to electron-electron repulsion pushing inner shells inward. Known exceptions (Cr: [Ar]3d⁵4s¹, Cu: [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹, and ~20 others for lanthanides and actinides) are substituted from a hard-coded table. Each subshell holds at most 2(2ℓ+1) electrons.

2 · Unsöld's theorem → full shells are isotropic

Σm=−ℓℓ |Yℓm(θ,φ)|² = 2ℓ+14π   — a constant, independent of direction
full shell (occ = 2(2ℓ+1)):   ρ(r,θ,φ) ∝ Rn,ℓ(r)² · 2ℓ+14π   — spherical
→ sample with uniform random direction on the sphere, no angular table needed

Unsöld's theorem (1927) is an exact result of spherical harmonics: when all 2ℓ+1 magnetic quantum numbers are equally occupied (as in a full subshell), the total angular density is a constant. The neon 2p⁶ shell is exactly spherical; the nitrogen 2p³ half-shell (one electron per m ∈ {−1, 0, +1}) is also spherical by the same argument — the three real spherical harmonics px, py, pz together cover all three spatial directions equally. Carbon's 2p² is not spherical and must use Hund's rule instead.

partial subshell?

3 · Hund's rule for partial subshells

assign one electron to each m = −ℓ, −ℓ+1, …, ℓ before pairing
example: C 2p²  →  m=−1 and m=0 occupied  →  anisotropic (no spherical symmetry)
example: N 2p³  →  all three m singly occupied  →  spherical (half-Unsöld)

Hund's first rule (maximum multiplicity) says electrons in a partially filled subshell prefer parallel spins, which forces them onto different orbitals. In practice this means each occupied m gets its own angular density profile — px, py, pz for p², p³; dz², dxz, dyz… for d³, etc. Each such orbital is sampled by the standard orbital sampler with the (ℓ, m)'s angular tables, not a uniform sphere. Without this, carbon's 2p² would incorrectly render as a sphere.

4 · Split the total point budget

Ng = ⌊N · wg / ΣW⌋   (weight = electron count for that group)
largest-remainder correction distributes leftovers so Σ Ng = N exactly

N (kAtomNumPoints) is the total point budget for this element. Each drawing group's weight is its electron count — a doubly-occupied full 2p subshell (weight 6) gets three times as many points as a singly-occupied 2s (weight 2). The largest-remainder method prevents the sum from drifting off by one or two points due to floor truncation.

6 · Build the radial inverse-CDF

density ∝ [r·R(r)]² = u(r)²

Isotropic group (full subshell)

trapezoid-rule CDF directly on the table's log-uniform grid

Oriented group (Hund's rule)

re-sample R(r) = u/r at 1001 evenly spaced r ∈ [0, maxR], then CDF on [r·R]²

Both branches integrate the same physical density u(r)². The oriented branch resamples on a linear grid first because the hydrogen angular sampler (which it shares) expects a linear-grid radial CDF — an asymmetry kept deliberately from the Python model. The isotropic branch stays on the log-grid and integrates directly for higher accuracy near r = 0.

8 · Identify the valence subshell

rref(n,ℓ) = P90({ri ∈ subshell})
outer = argmax(n,ℓ) rref   — the spatially outermost subshell

The P90 radius of each subshell's sampled points is a robust proxy for its spatial extent. The outermost subshell by this metric is used as the valence: it drives screen scaling (step 9) and gets the brighter color (step below). Note that for transition metals, 3d lies interior to 4s (Fe: 3d mode ≈ 43 pm, 4s mode ≈ 240 pm) — the P90 correctly identifies 4s as valence even though 3d has higher ℓ.

9 · Color by shell, emphasize valence

base = shellColor(n)   (K→Q spectroscopic palette, n = 1..7)
outer subshell: c′ = c + (255−c)·fbright   (pushed toward white)
inner subshells: c′ = c·(1−fdim)   (darkened)

Points are emitted in contiguous per-subshell runs (AtomSubshellRange), so the lerp is applied once per subshell, not per point. Without the dim/brighten split, the valence subshell — often a small fraction of the total point budget — would be nearly invisible against the much larger core shells.

10 · Scale calibration and screen fit

(x,y,z) ×= κZ   — Clementi–Raimondi size correction
scale = ptargetrrefouter   (ptarget = 96 px = 240 / 2.5)

The κZ factor corrects the LDA over-delocalization: it is the ratio of the Clementi-Raimondi literature valence radius to the LDA table's valence mode radius, computed once offline by atom_size_calib_gen.py. The subsequent divide by rrefouter then normalises all elements to the same on-screen target radius — like the orbital pipeline's P90 auto-zoom — so lithium and uranium are both legible on the 240 px display, at the cost of hiding the real size trend between elements.

Hydrogenic fallback — effective nuclear charge (Z > 92)

micropython/slater.py · micropython/slater_cr_zeff.py — also used for --model hydrogenic

Given — the hydrogen radial wavefunction

RHn,ℓ(r) = (Σk bk rk) · rℓ · e−r/n
mode of r²|RH|²  at  rmax ≈ n²/Z   a₀   (for an s orbital)

The exact analytic solution for Z = 1, N = 1. For Z > 1 it can still be reused by substituting an effective nuclear charge Zeff < Z in place of 1 — which compresses the wavefunction by a factor of Zeff, moving rmax to ≈ n²/Zeff a₀. The rest of the pipeline (CDF build, point sampling) is unchanged.

orbitals.py / orbitals.h — the validated hydrogen engine
choose Z_eff source

1 · Clementi–Raimondi table (Z ≤ 54, primary source)

ZeffCR(Z, n, ℓ) — published Hartree-Fock SCF values, Z = 1..54
source: Clementi & Raimondi 1963 (Z ≤ 36); Clementi, Raimondi & Reinhardt 1967 (Z = 37..54)

Clementi-Raimondi Zeff values are obtained by fitting the hydrogen-like wavefunction to the actual Hartree-Fock SCF orbital — they are therefore the best possible nuclear charge to use with the analytic hydrogen formula. Their advantage over Slater's rules is significant for the p-block (error drops from ~5% to <3% for period 2 p states). They cover all occupied subshells of elements 1 to 54. Beyond that, no published 3-parameter fit exists in this project's scope, so Slater's rules take over.

slater_cr_zeff.py / z_eff_cr()
Z > 54: Slater's rules

2 · Slater's shielding rules (Z > 54)

group electrons: [1s][2s,2p][3s,3p][3d][4s,4p][4d][4f]…
σ =
0.30 per electron   (same 1s group)
0.35 per electron   (same group, other than 1s)
0.85 per electron   (one group below, s/p subshell)
1.00 per electron   (two or more groups below)
Zeff = Z − Σ σ

Slater's rules (1930) capture the dominant physics of shielding: inner-core electrons (two or more groups below) screen the nuclear charge nearly completely, while electrons in the same subshell only partially screen each other (0.35, not 1.00), because they occupy the same spatial region rather than surrounding the nucleus. The d/f rule (1.00 for one group below) is a Slater-specific choice that slightly overestimates shielding for d electrons compared to the Clementi-Raimondi fit, but is simple and close enough for the fallback regime.

slater.z_eff() / shielding constants 0.30 / 0.35 / 0.85 / 1.00
n ≥ 4? apply n* correction

3 · The n* correction for outer shells (n ≥ 4)

n* =
3.7   (n=4)
4.0   (n=5)
4.2   (n=6)
4.4   (n=7)
Zeff,used = Zeff · n*n

The hydrogen radial wavefunction's mode radius scales as n² / Z. But Slater's Zeff was derived assuming the hydrogenic n is the real principal quantum number. For valence electrons in period 4 and beyond, the wavefunction must be orthogonal to the filled inner shells — this pushes the outer lobe inward. Slater introduced an "effective quantum number" n* < n to account for this: replacing n with n* in the mode-radius formula gives a smaller (more physical) radius. The code applies this as a Zeff rescaling (equivalent to n → n*) to keep the radial engine unchanged.

slater.n_star() / z_eff_radial()
Z_eff (corrected)

4 · The variable substitution r → Z_eff · r

Rn,ℓ(r; Zeff) = RHn,ℓ(Zeff · r)
mode of r²|R|² at   rmax ≈ n² / Zeff   a₀   (or n*² / Zeff with the correction)

The substitution contracts all distances by a factor of Zeff — the orbital shape (number of radial nodes, angular lobes) is unchanged, only the scale shrinks. In code this means passing z_eff as a multiplicative factor into init_orbital_sampler() or buildRadialSamplerRuntime(), which applies it when evaluating R(r) — the function orbitals.py itself is never modified and remains the single validated source of the hydrogenic math. One Zeff per subshell (not per individual electron) is the standard Slater mean-field approximation.

init_orbital_sampler(z_eff=…) / buildRadialSamplerRuntime(zEff)

Accuracy limits of the two models

atomSFE (Z ≤ 92) — default

eigenvalues: ≤ 7×10⁻⁶ Ha vs NIST LDA, 92/92 configs matched

LDA self-interaction error slightly over-delocalizes valence orbitals. Corrected by κZ against the Clementi–Raimondi literature radius at render time — the internal wavefunction shape is NIST-accurate, only the displayed scale is recalibrated.

Hydrogenic Z_eff (Z > 92 or --model hydrogenic)

period 2 p-block: ~1–3%; period 3–4: ~30–54%; period 6+: 3–5×

The hydrogenic functional form cannot capture the inner-node structure that compresses valence orbitals in heavy atoms. Clementi–Raimondi (Z ≤ 54) is significantly better than Slater; the n* correction recovers part of the gap for n ≥ 4, but residual error grows with Z. See ATOMS.md §4.2 for the per-element table.

Neither model includes relativistic effects (Dirac equation). Relevant for Z ≳ 55 — the contracted 6s orbital of gold responsible for its color and Au–Au bond strength cannot be reproduced without a relativistic treatment. Noted as a known limit; not planned for this project's scope.
